OMBS
Open Making & Building Standard K–12 Cross-Domain Framework

No music. Twenty seconds of the interface.

See what making and building well looks like at your child’s age.

Twenty seconds of the tool being used. It is free, it needs no account, it saves nothing, and the name you type never leaves your browser.

What happens in the twenty seconds

In the order the video does it. Every one of these is a control being operated, not a claim about an outcome.

  1. 01

    A first name and an age. The age derives the grade band and marks it on the slider. The name personalises the copy: “Lio is in this band”. Both are optional, and neither is stored.

  2. 02

    Up to three things they like making. The picks compute a lean — toward Making or toward Building — and each selected chip tints with that half’s colour. Below, the page narrows to the four dimensions that fit.

  3. 03

    Six practices, four grade bands. Define, Draft, Test, Iterate, Share, AI-Independence. Drag the slider and one practice matures across K–2, 3–5, 6–8 and 9–12. Slide off your child’s band and it says so — it does not re-place the child.

  4. 04

    The standard’s own words, with its code. Every band shows the verbatim descriptor and its identifier, so you can go and read the clause the sentence came from.

This is not a score, and OMBS never produces one.

OMBS records that a practice happened, tied to real work — never how well, never a level, never a rank. Nothing here places one child against another, nothing here is a test, and there is nothing to sign up for.

The tool is built live from the standard: 57 evidence descriptors across 14 dimensions in OMBS v0.3.0, plus an evidence layer of 6 evidence forms, 5 observer types and 6 admissibility rules. Those numbers are counted out of standards.json in the browser — nothing on the page types them in.
